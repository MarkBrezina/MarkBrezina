Proposed Execution Strategies
Here are your ideas, refined for a formal project description:
	• 1. Order Slicing & Scheduling This involves breaking a large parent order into smaller child orders that are executed over a defined time horizon. This is the foundation of most execution algorithms.
		○ Scheduled Execution (Your Idea 4.2): Orders are placed according to a pre-defined schedule. The most common benchmarks are:
			§ Time-Weighted Average Price (TWAP): Slices the order into equal portions executed at regular time intervals (e.g., 10% of the order every 30 minutes). This is simple but can be predictable.
			§ Volume-Weighted Average Price (VWAP): Slices the order in proportion to the historical or expected trading volume of the asset. This makes the trading activity look more like the natural market flow.
		○ Stochastic Execution (Your Idea 4.4): To counteract the predictability of simple schedules, this method introduces randomness into the timing and size of the child orders. While it follows a general schedule, the exact execution within each time slice is randomized to avoid detection by competing algorithms looking for simple TWAP/VWAP patterns.
	• 2. Signal-Driven Execution (Your Idea 4.3) Instead of executing based on time or volume alone, this strategy directly links the execution logic to the alpha-generating signal. The rate and size of trading are modulated by the strength or confidence of the trading signal. For example, the algorithm would trade more aggressively when the signal is strong and reduce its pace or even pause if the signal weakens.
	• 3. Multi-Account Fragmentation (Your Idea 4.1) This strategy involves distributing the parent order across multiple sub-accounts or even different brokers. By doing this, the total order size is obfuscated, making it significantly harder for other participants to reconstruct the full position and identify the underlying strategy.
	• 4. Game-Theoretic & Deceptive Tactics (Your Idea 4.5) Drawing inspiration from strategic games like chess or StarCraft, this approach uses counter-intuitive or deceptive methods to mislead competitors. The goal is to make your activity appear random or even contrary to your true intentions. A practical (and legal) example of this is using iceberg orders, which only display a small fraction of the total order size on the public order book, hiding the true depth of your interest.

Additional Ideas to Consider
Here are a few other industry-standard execution strategies that align with your goal of stealth:
	• 5. Liquidity-Seeking Algorithms (Dark Pools) These algorithms are designed to opportunistically find liquidity without signaling intent. A primary method is to route orders to dark pools—private exchanges where liquidity is not publicly displayed. The algorithm will "ping" these venues to find a match for the order off the public books, minimizing market impact.
	• 6. Implementation Shortfall (IS) Algorithms Also known as "arrival price" algorithms, these are more advanced strategies. The goal is to minimize the total execution cost (including market impact and price slippage) relative to the market price at the moment the decision to trade was made. These algorithms are adaptive; they will trade more aggressively when the price is moving against them and more passively when the price is moving in their favor.
	• 7. Adaptive & Intelligent Slicing This is a hybrid approach where the algorithm adapts its strategy in real-time based on market conditions. For example, it might:
		○ Increase participation during periods of high liquidity.
		○ Reduce its trading rate if it detects rising volatility or senses that its own orders are beginning to impact the price.
		○ Switch between passive (limit) and aggressive (market) orders based on the urgency and the state of the order book.
